# Test Against Reality

Use this skill when an idea has moved beyond explanation and needs contact with evidence.

## Operating premise

**When the plan stops working, the mission does not stop with it.**

A framework is not validated because it is coherent, persuasive, published, or repeated. Test what it claims against what actually happened.

This skill completes the Ed Reif Field System:

**BUILD → DECIDE → POSITION → TEST → UPDATE**

## Core loop

**CLAIM → FIELD → FRICTION → EVIDENCE → GAP → UPDATE**

### CLAIM — What are we saying should be true?
State the doctrine, assumption, prediction, design choice, AI output, procedure, or decision rule precisely enough to test.

### FIELD — Where does it meet reality?
Identify the actual or representative operating environment, actors, mission, conditions, constraints, and consequences.

### FRICTION — What challenged it?
Find ambiguity, unexpected behavior, competing signals, degraded resources, human factors, automation surprises, communication gaps, exceptions, or changed conditions.

### EVIDENCE — What actually happened?
Separate observations and source evidence from interpretation, recollection, inference, and outcome.

### GAP — Where did model and reality diverge?
Locate the mismatch. Determine whether the issue belongs to the doctrine, implementation, assumptions, environment, measurement, or evidence.

### UPDATE — What changes now?
Turn the lesson into a revised rule, scenario, threshold, field tool, question, evidence requirement, or explicit unresolved uncertainty.

## Workflow

### 1. State the claim
Rewrite vague claims into testable propositions.

### 2. Establish provenance
Identify where the claim came from and whether it is doctrine, evidence, inference, hypothesis, anecdote, or AI-generated synthesis.

### 3. Define the field
Specify the environment and population to which the claim is supposed to apply. Do not generalize evidence beyond its context without justification.

### 4. Hunt for friction
Actively seek exceptions, contradictory cases, edge conditions, failure modes, and circumstances where the claim becomes less useful.

### 5. Build the evidence record
Capture supporting, contradicting, ambiguous, and missing evidence separately.

### 6. Compare model with reality
Describe where expectations matched, partially matched, or diverged.

### 7. Diagnose the gap
Possible causes include:
- incorrect assumption;
- incomplete model;
- implementation failure;
- changed conditions;
- missing signal;
- wrong threshold;
- unrecognized limit;
- communication gap;
- automation or human-machine mismatch;
- measurement failure;
- evidence too weak to conclude.

### 8. Update proportionately
Do not rewrite doctrine because of one noisy observation. Do not protect doctrine from repeated contradictory evidence. Match the size of the update to the strength and relevance of evidence.

### 9. Convert learning into action
Create a changed scenario, decision cue, threshold, checklist item, escalation trigger, training condition, evidence requirement, or research question.

### 10. Preserve the trail
Record what changed, why it changed, the supporting evidence, remaining uncertainty, and what would trigger another update.

## Required output pattern

Unless the user requests another format, produce:

1. **Claim being tested**
2. **Provenance / status**
3. **Field context**
4. **Expected behavior**
5. **Observed evidence**
6. **Friction / contradictions**
7. **Gap diagnosis**
8. **Confidence and uncertainty**
9. **UPDATE**
10. **Next field test**

## Evidence status

Label important material where useful:

- **DOCUMENTED** — directly supported by a source or observation.
- **REPORTED** — stated by a participant or source but not independently verified.
- **INFERRED** — reasoned from available evidence.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — plausible but not yet adequately tested.
- **PROPOSED** — a design recommendation or future action.
- **UNKNOWN** — evidence is insufficient.

## Guardrails

- Do not manufacture evidence to complete a field note.
- Do not convert anecdote into a universal rule.
- Do not confuse absence of evidence with evidence of absence.
- Do not use hindsight as if later information was available during the event.
- Do not protect a favored framework from contradictory evidence.
- Do not overcorrect from a single noisy case.
- Preserve provenance and distinguish observation from interpretation.
- In safety-critical or regulated settings, authoritative records and qualified review take precedence.
- Treat AI synthesis as a hypothesis or analysis unless grounded in evidence.

## Relationship to the other skills

**Operational Readiness — BUILD:** prepare capability before the moment.

**Judgment at the Edge — DECIDE:** structure reasoning when the moment arrives.

**Design the Bet — POSITION:** structure exposure to uncertainty.

**Test Against Reality — TEST → UPDATE:** compare the system with evidence and feed learning back into the next version.
